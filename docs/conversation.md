# How the system talks to you

The system speaks on exactly four channels. Nothing else is allowed to interrupt
you. The philosophy: the system watches every day so you do not have to, and
speaks only when speaking is worth a fee and a decision.

From v10.7.1, every piece of advice you see — the briefing Actions table, the
`quant run` printout, the Overview steps, the weekly report, and the Monthly
decision screen — comes from ONE pipeline (`quant/engine/advice.py`) that obeys
the sizing laws. No screen computes its own buy or sell label, so the system can
never contradict itself, and a long-term asset can never be sold by any surface.

## 1. Daily silent monitor (every trading day, 18:45)

Runs the full pipeline: update market data, revalue holdings, recompute scores,
evaluate alert conditions. If no condition fires, it writes its artifacts and
says **nothing** to you. Silence is the default state.

## 2. Friday weekly summary

Five lines, generated by the Friday daily run:

1. open alerts and their status;
2. up to three notable score movements ("Amazon.com: tactical 58 to 64");
3. the regime line ("Market is rising, high confidence. Affects only the
   Active part.");
4. a monitoring-gap note if any days were missed;
5. if all empty: "Nothing urgent this week."

It appears as an in-app block on Overview, is included in the weekly report, and
is pushed to Telegram only if it contains an open alert or a gap note.

## 3. Monthly decision (your main ritual)

Once per month, before the savings plan executes, you enter the budget and
approve the split. This is 90 percent of your interaction with the system. See
the Monthly decision page.

### One target map, one answer

Every surface reads the SAME per-symbol invested-pool targets
(`TARGET_WEIGHTS_INVESTED` in `quant/config.py`, overridable per symbol by an
optional `target_pct` column in `data/tiers.csv`). The allocator, the advice
pipeline, the split reason lines, and the drift computations all use it, so two
screens can never give opposite advice.

Worked example. Invested pool 10,000 EUR: EUNL.DE 3,400 EUR (34 percent, target
50 percent), 5J50.DE 1,700 EUR (17 percent, target 10 percent), SXRV.DE 2,000
EUR (20 percent, target 20 percent), AMZN 2,900 EUR (29 percent, target 20
percent). Budget 200 EUR.

- Long base = 70 percent of 200 = 140 EUR. EUNL.DE is under target (gap 16
  points); 5J50.DE is over target (gap 0). The long base goes to EUNL.DE.
- The split reason states both numbers: "Global Aero & Defense is 17 percent of
  invested; its target is 10 percent; it is over target, so new long-term money
  goes to the under-target holding instead."
- `quant run` and the Overview steps name the SAME asset for the SAME reason:
  "Top up the savings plan for iShares Core MSCI World (EUNL.DE): it is 34
  percent of invested vs 50 percent target."

The old contradiction (advice topping up EUNL.DE while the allocator put money
into 5J50.DE using an equal-split-within-Fortress target) is resolved: there is
one target map, and the equal-split logic is removed.

## 4. Alerts (rare, urgent)

When a condition fires, the system pushes immediately and keeps the alert
visible everywhere until you resolve it. Alerts are phrased as actions for the
next trading day, because the broker accepts orders in the evening and executes
them at the next market open:

```
ACTION FOR TOMORROW
  Amazon.com: sell about 75 EUR.
  Place the order tonight or tomorrow; it executes at market open.
  Reason: tactics fell from 71 to 38 in 7 days.
  Fee: 1 EUR sell. Reply in app: done / declined with reason.
```

## When the system is silent

- No alert condition is true.
- The market is closed (weekends).
- Nothing changed since the last run.

Silence is not a failure. It means there is nothing worth a fee and a decision.

## Why level-triggered

The laptop sleeps. Edge-triggered alerts would be missed. Every condition
inspects the current state, so a monitoring gap delays an alert but never loses
it. The exact gap line is: "Monitoring gap: no runs for N days; conditions
evaluated on the latest data."

## Buffett filter (v10.7.6)

The system scores stocks on Buffett-style fundamental quality, a third lens
alongside the structural and tactical grades:

- high ROE and ROIC from genuine profitability;
- low debt (debt-to-equity below 1.0);
- a reasonable price (P/E below 25);
- stable earnings over three years;
- an economic moat (a durable competitive advantage).

A stock that meets at least four of the five criteria appears in the "Buffett
candidates" section on Find investments, and its score and moat appear in the
My holdings table and expander. The filter is additive: it never changes the
structural or tactical grade, and it is not applied to ETFs or commodities,
which have no company fundamentals.

## Tax accounting (v10.7.6)

The system tracks realized gains and dividends through the year, applies the
German Sparerpauschbetrag (1000 EUR tax-free allowance for a single filer,
2000 EUR for a married couple), and estimates the Abgeltungssteuer at 26.375
percent (25 percent plus the 5.5 percent solidarity surcharge).

The Tax summary page shows:

- realized gains and dividends for the year;
- how much of the tax-free allowance is used and how much remains;
- the taxable income and the estimated tax;
- losses you can use to lower tax (selling losers before year-end to offset
  gains).

Record a trade once, on My holdings (the quick-events form). The Tax summary
page reads the same ledger and links there.
